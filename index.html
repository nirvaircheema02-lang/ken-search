Executive Overview

Interview Context

This interview was conducted with Manish Israni, CTO, Westcon Comstor, for a busbar manufacturer looking to expand product sales into data centres. The discussion explains where busbars fit across the data-centre electrical architecture, especially main feeders, distribution rows and tap-off boxes, and why the commercial route is shaped by EPC consultants, system integrators and turnkey players rather than direct targeting of data-centre operators alone.

Entry Case Busbar manufacturer

Assessing how to expand its customer portfolio and target data centres for product-sales expansion.

Core Components Main feeder · Distribution rows · Tap-off boxes

Key areas where busbar systems support high-density power flow.

Decision Route EPC consultants · System integrators · Turnkey players

Key stakeholders influencing busbar vendor selection.

Ken Research Interpretation

Key Strategic Takeaways

Actionable implications derived from the expert conversation.

Busbar Manufacturers

Market access depends on certification readiness and ecosystem trust.

  • Certification is a market-access requirement.
  • Build vendor approval, integration comfort and trusted relationships.
  • Engage EPC consultants, system integrators and turnkey players rather than relying on direct sales.
  • Focus on main feeders, distribution rows and tap-off boxes as the key fitment areas.

EPCs and System Integrators

Modular busbars support faster and more flexible capacity expansion.

  • Enable plug-and-play expansion.
  • Support faster installation and easier maintenance.
  • Reduce rework during upgrades.
  • Extend capacity without redesigning the complete cable map.

Strategy and Market-Entry Teams

Demand is increasing, but approval and specification remain the entry barriers.

  • AI and GPU clusters are increasing data-centre power density.
  • Floor-level capacity has moved from around 1–3 MW toward nearly 20 MW.
  • The market behaves like a specification ecosystem, not a simple direct product sale.
  • Ecosystem acceptance may be a bigger barrier than manufacturing capability.
Expert Conversation

Interview Q&A

A conversation on power architecture, modular expansion, certifications and the commercial route into data centres.

Power Architecture and Busbar Fitment

Aakash Singh· Host
How is power distribution structured in modern data centres, and where do busbars fit in?
Manish Israni· Expert

Power typically moves from the transformer to the UPS, then to LT panels or distribution boxes, and finally into busbar systems. For a busbar manufacturer, the key fitment areas are the main feeder, distribution rows and tap-off boxes.

Busbars are preferred because they simplify high-current distribution and make future upgrades easier than cable-heavy layouts.

AI-led Power Density and Modularity

Aakash Singh· Host
How dramatically has power density changed in data centres, and what is driving it?
Manish Israni· Expert

The change is significant. Earlier, one data centre floor could operate at around 1–3 MW. Today, AI has picked up across the globe. This has increased computing requirements massively, resulting in nearly 20 MW of capacity being deployed on a single floor of a data centre.

This is being driven by GPU clusters, AI compute and higher rack density. At that load, cable upgrades become difficult to manage, which makes modular busbars far more practical.

“

Expert Commentary

“When a floor moves from 1–3 MW to nearly 20 MW, the question is no longer just cabling. The full power distribution architecture has to become more modular.”

Manish Israni

CTO, Westcon Comstor

Aakash Singh· Host
Why is modular busbar architecture preferred over traditional cabling?
Manish Israni· Expert

Modular busbars allow plug-and-play expansion, faster installation, easier maintenance and lower rework. If power demand increases, the system can be extended without redesigning the entire cable map.

That flexibility is important for data centre operators because power requirements can change quickly as workloads and rack densities increase.

Certification, Trust and Commercial Entry

Aakash Singh· Host
How important are certifications and standards in this market?
Manish Israni· Expert

Certifications are critical. IEC certification, IEEE certification, acceptance testing and standardisation carry strong weight because data centres are mission-critical environments.

For a new entrant, certification is not just paperwork. It is a market-access requirement that EPC consultants, system integrators and turnkey players have put in place.

Aakash Singh· Host
How does this market behave commercially? Is it a direct product sale?
Manish Israni· Expert

This is not a simple direct-to-customer product market. It behaves like a specification ecosystem where EPC Consultants, System Integrators and Turnkey Players, including UPS vendors as well, influence what gets approved.

Winning requires vendor approval, integration comfort and trusted relationships. For a busbar manufacturer entering data centres, ecosystem acceptance can be a bigger barrier than manufacturing capability.

“

Expert Commentary

“In data centres, a technically strong product still needs ecosystem approval. The market is won through specifications, certifications and trust.”

Manish Israni

CTO, Westcon Comstor
